HOW TO ADD WX NOW TO AN iPHONE
================================

Wx Now is a web app. It isn't in the App Store. You add it to the Home Screen from
Safari, and after that it opens and works like a normal app.

App address:  https://jlblan2.github.io/wx-now/

What you need
-------------
- An iPhone with iOS 16.4 or later (needed for rain alerts when the app is closed).
  Check under Settings > General > About > iOS Version.
- Safari. Other browsers on iPhone (Chrome, Firefox, etc.) can't add the app with
  alerts working, so use Safari for this setup.


PART 1 - ADD THE APP TO THE HOME SCREEN
---------------------------------------
1. Open Safari and go to:  https://jlblan2.github.io/wx-now/
2. When it asks to use your location, tap "Allow". The forecast is for where you
   are.
3. Tap the Share button: the square with an arrow pointing up. It's at the bottom of
   the screen, or at the top on some iPhones and iPads.
4. Scroll down the list and tap "Add to Home Screen".
   (If you don't see it, scroll to the bottom, tap "Edit Actions..." and add it.)
5. Leave the name as "Wx Now" and tap "Add" in the top-right corner.
6. A Wx Now icon (a blue raindrop on radar rings) appears on your Home Screen.

From now on, ALWAYS open the app from this icon, not from Safari. Rain alerts only
work when the app is opened from the Home Screen icon.


PART 2 - ALLOW LOCATION
-----------------------
1. Open Wx Now from the Home Screen icon.
2. When it asks to use your location, tap "Allow While Using App".
   If you said no by mistake: Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services >
   Safari Websites > set to "While Using the App", then reopen Wx Now.


PART 3 - TURN ON RAIN ALERTS (works even when the app is closed)
---------------------------------------------------------------
1. Open Wx Now from the Home Screen icon.
2. Tap the gear icon at the top right to open Settings.
3. Under "Alerts when the app is closed", tap "Turn on".
4. When iPhone asks to allow notifications, tap "Allow".
5. The label next to the heading changes to "On".
6. Tap "Send test alert". A notification should arrive within a few seconds.

You will then get an alert about 15 minutes before rain:
  - at your saved places (see Part 4), and
  - at the last place where you opened the app.
You get one alert per rain event per place, not repeated reminders.

With alerts on you also get hurricane alerts for the same places: when a place comes
inside a storm's forecast cone, when a watch or warning covers the nearby coast (and
again if it is upgraded), and when tropical-storm- or hurricane-force winds arrive.

Marine alerts: with "Include marine alerts" ticked in Settings (on by
default) you also get an alert when the NWS issues a Small Craft Advisory, Gale,
Storm, Hurricane Force Wind or Hazardous Seas watch/warning, or a Special Marine
Warning, for the zone picked in the marine forecast card or the marine zone at your saved
places and last location (when they are near the water). Each one alerts once, and an
upgrade (say, Small Craft Advisory to Gale Warning) alerts again.
Offshore and open-ocean zones (Gulf offshore, Caribbean, Bahamas and SW Atlantic,
Mid-Atlantic and New England offshore) alert on the gale, storm, hurricane-force,
tropical and hazardous-seas headlines in the NWS Offshore Waters Forecasts, e.g.
"Gale Conditions Possible Sun Night". Each headline alerts once while it stays in the
forecast, and again if it changes or comes back.

Tropical outlook alerts: with "Include tropical outlook alerts" ticked in Settings
(on by default) you get an alert when NHC starts watching a new Atlantic area, and
when an area's chance of becoming a tropical cyclone goes up (its 7-day chance rises
to medium or high, or its 2-day chance becomes high). Each alert says how far the
area is from your nearest saved place, e.g. "About 3,550 mi E of Home". Tapping it
opens the Hurricanes tab.

High water alerts: Settings > "High water alerts" (1 ft by default; also 0.5, 1.5,
2 ft or Off). The server checks the NOAA water level gauge nearest each saved place
and your last location (within 30 nm) every 6 minutes. You get an alert when the
measured water runs that far above the predicted tide, another if it climbs a further
foot, and one when it reaches NOAA's minor, moderate or major coastal flood level.
It stays quiet until the water drops back. Tapping it opens Seas & swell.

Tornado alerts: Settings > "Tornado alerts" (everything by default; or warnings only, or
Off). For each saved place and your last location, checked every 2 minutes:
- National Weather Service Tornado Warnings. When the warning covers the place:
  "TORNADO WARNING - Home: Take shelter now: lowest floor, small interior room, away from
  windows..." with whether the tornado was observed or seen on radar, and how fast and
  which way it is moving. When a warning is within 15 miles but does not cover the place
  yet: "Tornado warning nearby - Home: Tornado warning 11 mi W of Home ... moving E at
  30 mph, heading toward Home. Be ready to take shelter." If the warning later covers the
  place, or is upgraded to a "particularly dangerous" warning or a TORNADO EMERGENCY, you
  get another alert. The NWS updates for the same warning do not repeat it.
- Tornado Watches for the place (marked PDS when the NWS calls it a particularly
  dangerous situation).
- The Storm Prediction Center's daily tornado outlook: a heads-up when a place has a 5%
  or higher chance of a tornado within 25 miles that day (again if the chance goes up).
Waterspouts over the water come in Special Marine Warnings with the marine alerts.
Tornado watches and warnings were part of the lightning and thunderstorm alerts before;
they now come only with these tornado alerts.

Hail alerts: Settings > "Hail alerts" (1 inch, quarter size, by default; also 0.75 in
penny, 1.75 in golf ball, 2.75 in baseball, or Off). For each saved place and your last
location:
- Hail on radar, checked every 4 minutes: NOAA's radar estimate of the largest hail in the
  last 30 minutes (MRMS). You get an alert when hail of your size is within 10 miles, for
  example "Hail detected - Home: Radar estimates hail up to 1.25 in (half dollar) 6 mi N
  of Home...", another if much larger hail shows up, then nothing more until an hour
  passes without hail. Radar estimates can run a little high.
- National Weather Service Severe Thunderstorm and Tornado Warnings that expect hail of
  your size. The warning's hail size is added to lightning/thunderstorm alerts too.
- The Storm Prediction Center's daily hail outlook: a heads-up when a place is in an area
  with a 15% or higher chance of hail 1 inch or larger within 25 miles that day (again if
  the chance goes up), so you can get cars and boats under cover ahead of time.
Radar hail covers the US mainland and nearby waters, not the Bahamas or Caribbean.

Rip current alerts: Settings > "Rip current alerts" (High risk by default; also Moderate
or high, or Off). Every hour the server reads the National Weather Service Surf Zone
Forecast for the beach zone at each saved place and your last location: the rip current
risk (low, moderate or high) and surf height for today and tomorrow. You get an alert when
the risk reaches your level, for example "High rip current risk - Home: Sunday: high, surf
9 to 11 feet...", another if it rises from moderate to high, and none again until it has
dropped back. High Surf Advisories and Warnings and Beach Hazards Statements for the place
come too. Places inland or on a bay have no surf zone, so they get no rip current alerts.

Lightning and thunderstorm alerts: Settings > "Lightning and thunderstorm alerts"
(10 miles by default; also 5, 15, 20, 30 miles, or Off). For each saved place and your last
location, every 5 minutes:
- Lightning detected by NOAA's GOES-19 weather satellite (its Lightning Mapper sees every
  flash, day and night, over land and sea). You get "Lightning nearby" when 2 or more
  flashes come within your distance in 15 minutes, with how far away and which direction;
  "Lightning very close" if one comes within 3 miles; and "Lightning all clear" after 30
  minutes with no lightning in range (the 30-30 rule). The satellite places flashes to
  within a few miles, so treat distances as approximate.
- National Weather Service Severe Thunderstorm Watches and Warnings for the place.
Special Marine Warnings (thunderstorms over the water) come with the marine alerts.

Fog alerts: Settings > "Fog alerts" (1 nm by default; also 2, 0.5, 0.25 nm, or Off).
Every 30 minutes the server checks two things for each saved place:
- the hourly visibility forecast for the next 12 hours: you get an alert when visibility
  is forecast to drop to your distance for 2 hours or more, for example "Fog forecast -
  Home: Visibility down to 0.15 nm (300 yd), Sun 4 AM to Sun 9 AM. Lowest around Sun 6 AM."
  After that it stays quiet until the forecast clears;
- National Weather Service Dense Fog, Freezing Fog and Dense Smoke Advisories issued for
  the place itself.
Marine alerts now include Dense Fog Advisories for your marine zones too (sent once even
if both cover the same advisory).

Extreme tide alerts: Settings > "Extreme tide alerts" (1 ft by default; also 0.5, 1.5,
2 ft, or Off). Every 3 hours the server reads NOAA's tide predictions for the next 2 days
at the tide station nearest each saved place (within 30 nm). You get an alert ahead of time
when a high tide is predicted to run that far above a normal high (Mean Higher-High Water)
or to reach the minor flooding level, for example king tides; or when a low tide is
predicted that far below a normal low (Mean Lower-Low Water), which matters for depths at
docks, channels and inlets. At most one alert of each kind per station a day. These are
predictions from the moon and sun only; wind and storms add to them (see High water alerts).

Wind forecast alerts: Settings > "Wind forecast alerts" (25 kt by default; also 15, 20,
30 kt, 34 kt gale, 48 kt storm, or Off). For each saved place the server checks the hourly
wind forecast every hour. You get an alert when sustained wind in the next 24 hours is
forecast to reach your speed, for example "Wind forecast - Home: Up to 28 kt (32 mph) from
the NE, gusts to 39 kt, Sat 7 PM to Sun 3 AM. Strongest Sat 11 PM." You get another if the
forecast grows 10 kt stronger. After that it stays quiet until the forecast eases. Saved
places only, not your last location.

Water temperature alerts: Settings > "Water temperature alerts" (3 F by default; also
2, 5 or 9 F, or Off). The server watches the NOAA buoy or coastal station nearest each
saved place and your last location (within 50 nm), checking every 30 minutes. Its first
reading is the starting point; when the water has warmed or cooled by your amount from
it (on two reports in a row, so one bad reading doesn't set it off), you get an alert
such as "Water cooler - WEST TAMPA: 79 F, down 4 F since Tue, Oct 13 (83 F)", and that
reading becomes the new starting point. Alerts use your units setting (F or C).


PART 4 - SAVE PLACES (optional)
-------------------------------
Save places such as Home or the marina to switch to them with one tap, and to get
alerts there wherever you are.
1. Get the forecast showing the place you want:
     - For where you are now, just open the app.
     - For somewhere else, tap that spot on the Local weather map.
2. Tap the + button at the end of the place row under the tabs (or open Settings,
   the gear icon). Under "Saved places", type a name (for example "Home").
3. Tap "Save this spot".
4. Your saved places appear as buttons under the tabs. Tap one to switch the whole
   app to it; tap "My location" to go back to your GPS position.
5. To remove a place, tap "Remove" next to it in Settings.
You can save up to 10 places.


USING THE APP
-------------
Three tabs under the title keep the screen uncluttered; the app remembers the last
one you used. Each tab has its own forecasts:
  Local weather  - rain in the next hour, the radar map, next 24 hours, 7 days, the NOAA vs
                   Navy second opinion and current details.
  Hurricanes     - active Atlantic storms (or a note when there are none), NHC's
                   tropical weather outlook, the storm map and storm surge maps.
  Seas & swell   - your marine zone forecast, tides, water temperature, the sailing
                   weather charts and
                   the Navy wave map.
Under the tabs is a row of places: "My location" (your GPS) and each saved place.
Tap one to switch the whole app - forecast, radar, marine zone and tides - to that
place; the app remembers the last place you chose. The + button opens Settings to
save the spot you are looking at as a new place.
All times in the app are local time at the place shown, not your phone's time zone:
Reykjavik shows Iceland time, Mobile shows Central time, and a spot out at sea shows its
nautical time zone (for example GMT-6 in the central Gulf). Details > "Local time" shows
the time there now, and notes when it differs from your phone. Alerts use the same rule.
A slider on the right edge of the screen shows where you are on the page: drag it,
or tap the track, to scroll quickly up and down.
The map at the bottom of each tab shows only that tab's layers. Tapping a hurricane
alert opens the Hurricanes tab; tapping a marine alert opens Seas & swell.

- When there's an active Atlantic hurricane or tropical storm, a "Tropical storms" card appears
  under the forecast. For each storm it shows strength, movement, how far it is from
  you, its closest forecast approach, and whether you are in the forecast cone or near
  a coastal watch or warning. "Show on map" zooms to the storm: cone (white), forecast
  track with points coloured by strength, past track, wind field (yellow = tropical-
  storm force, red = hurricane force) and coastal watches/warnings (yellow = tropical
  storm watch, blue = tropical storm warning, pink = hurricane watch, red = hurricane
  warning). Untick "on map" in the card to hide the storm drawing.
- Tropical weather outlook (Hurricanes tab): NHC's latest Atlantic outlook, issued
  every 6 hours, with each area NHC is watching and its chance of becoming a tropical
  cyclone in the next 2 and 7 days (yellow = low, orange = medium, red = high). The
  same areas are shaded on the storm map, with arrows showing where they are expected
  to move and an X at their present position.
- Storm surge: the "Storm surge" menu under the map shows NHC's storm surge maps.
  "If a Category 1-5 hurricane hits" are planning maps: the worst flooding NHC expects
  for that strength (blue = up to 3 ft above ground, yellow = over 3 ft, orange = over
  6 ft, red = over 9 ft). During an Atlantic storm that threatens the US, a "Live
  forecast" choice appears with NHC's actual surge forecast. For coastal locations the
  details at the bottom also list "Storm surge risk (NHC)" by hurricane category.
- The headline gives the next hour in plain words, with the local time of each change
  and how likely that time is to be right, for example
  "Light rain starting in 12 min (2:42 PM - 85% on time), stopping 28 min later
  (3:10 PM - 85% on time)". "On time" means within 5 minutes of the time shown. It is
  high when radar shows rain arriving or ending sharply, and low when the rain builds or
  fades gradually, so the exact minute is uncertain.
- The bar chart shows rain minute by minute. Faded bars mean the forecast is less
  certain; the dashed orange line is the chance of rain.
- "Next 24 hours" scrolls sideways. "7 days" shows the week ahead.
- Radar map: press Play to see the last 30 minutes and the next 60. The dashed line
  shows where your next hour of weather is coming from.
- White arrows on the map show which way the rain is moving, tracked from radar
  images. Teal arrows (one at each radar site, with a dot) show the wind measured by
  that radar's Doppler at 10,000-20,000 ft, the height that carries rain. Both use the
  same scale: an arrow's length is how far the rain or wind travels in 15 minutes.
  When the two agree, the forecast is on firm ground. Tap a teal arrow for its numbers.
  The arrow button at the right of the map's play bar turns both on and off.
- The details at the bottom list "Storm motion (tracked)" and "Doppler wind" side by
  side.
- The "V" button on the map's play bar switches to the red/green Doppler velocity map
  from the nearest radar: green is moving toward the radar, red away from it, purple
  is unresolved. It only shows motion along the radar beam; bright red right next to
  bright green means strong rotation or a sharp wind shift. Tap "V" again to go back
  to the rain map.
- Tap the refresh arrow at the top to update. The app also refreshes every 5 minutes
  while it is open.
- "Marine forecast" (below the map) shows the National Weather Service forecast for
  the Gulf or Atlantic coast marine zone nearest you: wind, seas and bay conditions for the next
  few days, with any Small Craft Advisory, Gale Warning or other marine alert shown in
  colour at the top. Tap a period for the full NWS text. Use the menu to pick another
  zone (listed nearest first, distances in nautical miles); "Show zone on map" outlines
  it. Offshore and open-ocean zones (Gulf offshore, Caribbean, the Bahamas and SW
  Atlantic, Mid-Atlantic and New England offshore) come from the NWS Offshore Waters
  Forecasts. Bermuda's own waters are forecast by the Bermuda Weather Service
  (weather.bm), which the card links to near Bermuda. Note: the NWS now calls it the
  Gulf of America in its forecasts.
- "Second opinion: NOAA vs Navy" compares NOAA's forecast models (GFS, GFS-Wave)
  with the U.S. Navy's (NAVGEM and WAVEWATCH III from Fleet Numerical Meteorology and
  Oceanography Center) for the next 5 days: high, low, rain, strongest wind and, on the
  water, highest waves. In each box the top number is NOAA and the lower is the Navy.
  Orange marks a disagreement; when the two agree, the forecast is more trustworthy.
- "Navy waves" (under the map) shows the Navy's wave height forecast in colour, with
  white arrows for the direction the waves are travelling. Drag its slider to step
  through the next 5 days.
- "Tides" (Seas & swell) shows NOAA tide predictions for the nearest tide station:
  whether the tide is rising or falling and its height now, the next high and low,
  a 3-day tide curve and each day's highs and lows. Pick another station from the
  menu (listed nearest first). Heights are above mean lower low water (MLLW), the
  datum on nautical charts.
  Below the tide line, "Measured" shows the latest real water level from the nearest
  NOAA gauge (updated every 6 minutes) and how far it is above or below the
  prediction - the effect of wind, pressure and storm surge. When the gauge is the
  station itself, the chart adds the measured level as an orange line over the blue
  predicted curve. Subordinate stations borrow the nearest gauge within 30 nm.
- "Water temperature" (Seas & swell) shows the sea surface temperature at your spot
  with its range over the next 5 days (Open-Meteo marine model), then the nearest NOAA
  buoys and coastal stations within 250 nm that measure water temperature: distance
  and direction, how long ago they reported, water and air temperature, waves and wind.
- "Sailing weather" is a sailing forecast chart for the US East Coast, the Gulf
  of America, the Bahamas, the Caribbean and Bermuda. Pick a region (Gulf, Florida,
  Bahamas, Caribbean, Western or Eastern Caribbean, Bermuda, the East Coast to Bermuda
  and Bermuda to Caribbean passages, Southeast, Mid-Atlantic, New England or the whole
  area) and a chart:
    Wind - colours by speed with standard wind barbs in knots (each full feather is 10
           kt, half feather 5 kt, triangle 50 kt; the barb points where the wind comes from)
    Waves - wave height with arrows showing which way the waves are travelling
    Pressure & rain - isobars every 4 mb with rain shading
    Visibility - fog and poor-visibility areas
    Sea temperature - NOAA GFS sea surface temperature (always NOAA; the Navy files
           have none), with a dot at every buoy measuring water temperature, labelled
           in degrees when zoomed in. Tap a dot for its water and air temperature,
           waves and wind.
    Water levels (measured now) - a dot at each NOAA water level gauge, coloured and
           labelled by how far the water is above (+) or below (-) the predicted tide
           (orange/red = high water, blue = low). A red ring means the gauge is at or
           above its minor flood level. Readings are refreshed every 30 minutes; tap a
           dot for the level, time, trend and flood level.
  Drag the time slider or press play to step through the next 7 days (NOAA) or 5 days
  (Navy; switch with the model menu). Tap anywhere for wind, gusts, waves, period,
  pressure, rain and visibility at that spot.
- Settings > Units switches between US units (inches, mph, F) and metric.


TROUBLESHOOTING
---------------
"No alert server is set up yet" when turning on alerts:
    The phone is using an old copy of the app. Swipe the app away to close it fully,
    wait 10 minutes and reopen it. Or open Settings > Advanced and type
    https://129-80-206-181.sslip.io  in "Alert server address", then tap "Turn on".

"Notifications are blocked":
    iPhone Settings > Notifications > Wx Now > turn on "Allow Notifications".
    Then turn alerts off and on again in the app.

"Open the app from its Home Screen icon":
    You opened it in Safari. Close Safari and open the Wx Now icon instead.

The app looks out of date after an update:
    Swipe it away to close it fully and reopen it. If that doesn't work, wait 10
    minutes and try again.

No alerts arriving:
    Open Settings in the app and tap "Send test alert". If the test arrives, alerts
    work, and there has simply been no rain heading your way. If it doesn't arrive,
    check that Focus / Do Not Disturb is off and notifications are allowed for
    Wx Now.

Setting up on a new or second iPhone:
    Repeat Parts 1 to 4 on that phone. Saved places are stored on each phone
    separately.


Note: Wx Now is not an official forecast. For warnings, check the National Weather
Service.

COVERAGE
--------
Wx Now covers the Atlantic side: the US East Coast, the Gulf of America, the
Bahamas, the Caribbean and Bermuda. Pacific storms, Pacific storm-surge maps and
Pacific Ocean wind, wave and pressure data are not included. The rain forecast,
hourly and 7-day forecasts still work anywhere in the US.
